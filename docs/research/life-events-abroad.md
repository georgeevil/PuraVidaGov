# Life events abroad — how other portals organise services, and what PuraVidaGov is missing

Research date: 7 September 2026. Written for the PuraVidaGov demo (portal → orchestrator → interoperability bus →
mock agencies). Purpose: choose the next life events to add, by looking at which events other countries have found
high-friction enough to organise their portals around.

Method: eight parallel researchers (one per country/framework) fetched the portals and agency pages directly and were
told that "[not verified]" is a correct answer. **No independent fidelity pass has been run on this version**: sections
1–6 were assembled by the coordinator under time pressure from pages the researchers had cached; before any
`LegalRef` or UI copy cites this file, a checker should open each URL. The URL follows each claim. Items that could not be confirmed are marked **not verified**. Foreign-language
labels are quoted in the original with an English gloss. The Russia section is a reference for the *organisation by
life situations* only, not a political endorsement.

Confidence is uneven and is stated per section: sections 7 (Singapore) and 8 (frameworks) were fully drafted and
sourced by their researchers; sections 1–6 were assembled by the coordinator from the pages the researchers had
already cached locally plus a handful of direct fetches, after the shared web-search quota ran out (200/200) and the
six country researchers had not reported. Several official sites (regjeringen.no, digdir.no, norge.no, vm.fi, dvv.fi,
mitid.dk, riigikontroll.ee, gosuslugi.ru, government.ru on re-fetch) answered 403/502 or a JavaScript shell from this
environment; where a fact rests on a page the researcher cached rather than one re-opened by the coordinator, the
entry says so.

## 1. Russia — Gosuslugi (gosuslugi.ru) — "жизненные ситуации"

## 3. Norway — the seven "livshendelser" (Digdir / norge.no / Altinn)

- **Source.** Kommunal- og moderniseringsdepartementet, *Én digital offentlig sektor — Digitaliseringsstrategi for
  offentlig sektor 2019–2025*, foreword dated "Oslo, 11. juni 2019" (PDF read from the researcher's cached copy;
  landing page https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/en-digital-offentlig-sektor/id2653874/ returned 403 on
  re-fetch, so the URL is **not re-verified**). Verbatim, with the responsible ministry in brackets as printed:
  > "I første omgang prioritere å utvikle sammenhengende tjenester innenfor følgende livshendelser:
  > – Få barn (ASD) – Alvorlig sykt barn (HOD) – Miste og finne jobb (ASD) – Ny i Norge (KD) – Dødsfall og arv (KMD)
  > – Starte og drive en frivillig organisasjon (KUD) – Starte og drive en bedrift (NFD)"
  So the second event is *seriously ill **child*** (not generic serious illness) and the seventh is *starting and
  running a voluntary organisation*. Rationale: "Det er valgt livshendelser som gjelder viktige situasjoner som berører
  de aller fleste i løpet av et liv, som det å få barn, dødsfall og arv." The strategy cites the EU eGovernment
  Benchmark as its yardstick (footnote 7).
- **Governance.** Digdir's report "Godt i gang med livshendelsene": "prioriterer vi sju livshendelser fra regjeringens
  digitaliseringsstrategi. Digdir samarbeider tett med KS om å koordinere og støtte arbeidet med livshendelsene, og har
  selv ansvaret for hendelsen dødsfall og arv". The same report says (paraphrase of column-broken text) that
  responsibility for each of the seven events sits with a ministry, which has delegated the work to a state agency;
  for Dødsfall og arv "Prosjektet fikk 13 millioner kroner fra Medfinansieringsordningen"; Ny i Norge: IMDi ran "rundt 300 intervjuer"; the voluntary-
  organisation event started as a Stimulab project (cached copy of the Digdir document; canonical digdir.no URL

- **2024 follow-up strategy** "Fremtidens digitale Norge" — **not verified** (403).

## 7. Singapore — LifeSG (formerly Moments of Life)

Draft written 2026-09-08 (SGT). Sources are inline. "[not verified]" marks claims that could not be confirmed against a primary page.

### 1. History

- **Strategic National Project.** On 21 August 2017 GovTech listed five Strategic National Projects: National Digital Identity, e-Payments, Smart Nation Sensor Platform, Smart Urban Mobility and Moments of Life. Moments of Life "bundles relevant government services, across different agencies, to the citizen at key moments of his life." https://www.tech.gov.sg/media/strategic-national-projects-to-build-a-smart-nation/
- **Pilot announced, March 2018.** A Smart Nation and Digital Government Office (Prime Minister's Office) media factsheet describes "the Moments of Life (MOL) initiative – one of Smart Nation's five strategic national projects" and states "From June 2018, the pilot MOL (Families) app will be rolled out to support every young child from birth." ### 4. Organisational model

- **Owner.** LifeSG is a GovTech product ("Product and services > For citizens > Digital services > LifeSG") and "one of the Strategic National Projects under Singapore's Smart Nation initiative." https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/digital-services/lifesg/ The 2020 relaunch was announced by the Smart Nation and Digital Government Group (SNDGG), comprising the Smart Nation and Digital Government Office and GovTech. https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/launch-of-lifesg-app/ The 2018 pilot factsheet was issued by the Smart Nation and Digital Government Office, Prime Minister's Office, with the media contact at the Public Service Division. https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/38/57ee7eb3-c7bf-4c2d-90c1-1914d8161240/moments-of-life-families-factsheet.pdf The developer portal describes LifeSG as "A Digital Solution That Simplifies Citizens' Interactions With the Government During Key Life Moments" https://www.developer.tech.gov.sg/products/categories/platform/lifesg/getting-started
- **Singpass as the login.** "Simply download the app and login with your Singpass to access the various services." https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/digital-services/lifesg/ "Your profile is set up instantly with Myinfo." https://www.life.gov.sg/about-us
- **Myinfo (tell-us-once).** GovTech, 26 Sep 2017: "users of MyInfo need only provide their personal details once to the Government"; "SingPass acts as an authentication gateway, while the MyInfo service provides the user's basic personal data"; 24 services at launch, target 140 government digital services by 2018. https://www.tech.gov.sg/media/no-more-repetitive-form-filling-after-you-tell-us-once-for-government-services/ MDDI 2022: "Myinfo enables users to pre-fill digital forms with their personal data from government sources for online transactions, while giving them control over how their information is shared." https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/singpass-factsheet-02032022/
- **Public Sector (Governance) Act 2018.** MDDI, 12 Jan 2026: "In 2018, Parliament passed the PSGA to establish a legal framework for public agencies to share data with one another for seven purposes supporting the public interest", including to "support a whole-of-government approach in the discharge of the functions of Singapore public sector agencies"; backend data sharing under it "facilitated the automatic qualification and disbursement for various support schemes, such as the CDC Vouchers scheme and the Majulah Package." The Public Sector (Governance) (Amendment) Bill passed 12 Jan 2026 extends sharing to "trusted partners" outside government under ministerial authorisation and criminal liability for misuse. https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/amendments-to-public-sector--governance--act-to-better-serve-singaporeans-through-enhanced-use-of-data/ No official page opened states that LifeSG or Myinfo depend on the PSGA specifically; treat that causal link as [not verified].
- **Government Data Architecture (GDA).** Civil Service College Ethos, July 2019: "SSOTs [Single Sources of Truth] are authoritative sources for core government data fields. They are tasked with maintaining, cleaning, and distributing these data fields to requesting agencies within seven working days"; "TCs [Trusted Centres] aggregate data across the SSOTs and provide a one-stop-shop for users to access core government datasets." https://knowledge.csc.gov.sg/ethos-issue-21/bring-data-in-the-heart-of-digital-government/ The article does not mention the PSGA.
- **Whole-of-Government framing.** The 2018 pilot factsheet: government services "designed based on key moments in people's lives, instead of how government agencies are set up" (paraphrase of the initiative description at https://www.life.gov.sg/rebrand and the pilot factsheet); the 2021 factsheet: "Information is grouped according to topics of interest … and not according to agencies." https://assets.life.gov.sg/lifesg/factsheets/lifesg-government-benefits-april.pdf

#### 1. EU Single Digital Gateway, Regulation (EU) 2018/1724

**Instrument.** Regulation (EU) 2018/1724 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 2 October 2018 establishing a single digital gateway to provide access to information, to procedures and to assistance and problem-solving services and amending Regulation (EU) No 1024/2012, OJ L 295, 21.11.2018, p. 1 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2018/1724/oj).

**Gateway and Your Europe.** Article 2(1): "A single digital gateway ('the gateway') shall be established by the Commission and the Member States in accordance with this Regulation. The gateway shall consist of a common user interface managed by the Commission ('the common user interface'), which shall be integrated into the Your Europe portal and shall give access to relevant Union and national webpages." (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32018R1724). The Your Europe portal itself (https://europa.eu/youreurope/index_en.htm) groups citizen content under Travel, Work and retirement, Vehicles, Residence, Education & Youth, Health, Family, Consumers, and business content under Business lifecycle, Growing a business, Selling in the EU, Hiring and managing staff, Finance & tax, Product rules and compliance, Governance & sustainability (category names as read from the portal navigation via fetch of that URL).

**"Life events" in the recitals.** Recital (4): "The gateway could contribute to the greater transparency of rules and regulations relating to different business and life events, in areas such as travel, retirement, education, employment, healthcare, consumer rights and family rights." (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32018R1724).

**Annex I versus Annex II.** They are different things:

- **Annex I** is the "List of areas of information relevant for citizens and business exercising their internal market rights referred to in point (a) of Article 2(2)". It is an information obligation, split into "Areas of information related to citizens" (A. Travel within the Union; B. Work and retirement within the Union; C. Vehicles in the Union; D. Residence in another Member State; E. Education or traineeship in another Member State; F. Healthcare; G. Citizens' and family rights; H. Consumer rights; I. Protection of personal data) and "Areas of information related to businesses" (J. Starting, running and closing a business; K. Employees; L. Taxes; M. Goods; N. Services; O. Funding a business; P. Public contracts; Q. Health and safety at work). (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32018R1724)
- **Annex II** is the "Procedures referred to in Article 6(1)", the ones that must be completable fully online. It is a three-column table: "Life events" / "Procedures" / "Expected output subject to an assessment of the application by the competent authority in accordance with national law, where relevant".

- User-driven: "A government becomes more user-driven by awarding a central role to people' needs and convenience in the shaping of processes, services and policies; and by adopting inclusive mechanisms for this to happen."

- Reactive versus proactive: "Through intelligent reuse of data and information previously generated and/or provided by citizens, governments can shift from reactive service delivery approaches to proactive practices (see section 7). - Proactiveness: "A proactive government pre-empts requests from citizens, instead providing answers or solutions to their needs through the adoption of push vs pull delivery models (Linders, Liao and Wang, 2018) that limit to the minimum the burdens and frictions of interacting with public sector organisations.

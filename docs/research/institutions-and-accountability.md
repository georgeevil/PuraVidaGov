# Who has to move, where it stands, and what a resident can do about it

**Evidence tags** follow the project convention: **[V]** verified against a primary source already cited in
`docs/research/legal-cr.md` or opened here; **[P]** press or secondary source; **[?]** stated but not verified —
do not repeat it on the site without checking first.

**Cut-off: 8 September 2026.** Two of the most important sources could not be reached from the build environment
at all (see §4), so this document is deliberately incomplete in a named way rather than silently.

## 1. The institutional map

The question "who should be implementing the laws needed" has an awkward answer: **for the once-only rule, nobody
is, because the law does not exist yet.** What exists is a policy project with no statutory duty behind it. So the
map has two halves — bodies executing something today, and the body that has not acted.

### 1.1 Rector and executor

| Body | Legal basis | What it is supposed to do | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MICITT**, Dirección de Gobernanza Digital | Rectoría over digital government; CNTD, Sello de Gobierno Digital | Set policy, standards and the national interoperability model | Published the interoperability model as a technical report, `MICITT-DGDCFD-INF-014-2025` — a report, **not a norm** **[V]** |
| **ANGD** — Agencia Nacional de Gobierno Digital | **Ley 9943** (2021), exp. 21.180; órgano de desconcentración mínima attached to MICITT; Junta Directiva of five (MICITT presiding, Hacienda, MEIC, MIDEPLAN, UCCAEP); financed inter alia by 10 % of central-government under-execution in ICT lines (art. 7) | Execute digital-government projects for public administration | Created by law in 2021 but **only regulated after a Sala IV order in July 2024** (Decreto 44636-MP-MICITT) and **formally operating since September 2025** **[V]**. Four years between the statute and an operating agency is the single most useful fact in this document — see §3. |
| **Conecta (CRI/003)** | No statute. A cooperation project, financed by Luxembourg, executed by ANGD with LuxDev | Adopt a country-wide X-Road interoperability model | Launched **11 March 2026**; ANGD coordinating **30 public institutions** **[P]**. First functional health-sector interoperability demonstration reported **June 2026** **[P]** |

**The key structural point, and it is load-bearing for everything below:** Ley 9943 art. 2 obliges institutions to
produce *planes de trabajo*, **not** to adhere to a national platform **[V]**. Conecta is therefore administrative,
not compulsory. An institution that simply does not join is not breaking any law.

### 1.2 The body that enforces the rule that already exists

| Body | Legal basis | Duty | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MEIC**, Dirección de Mejora Regulatoria | **Ley 8220** art. 11 (rectoría), art. 13 (criterio vinculante) | Stop institutions demanding documents another institution already holds (art. 2), and run the Catálogo Nacional de Trámites | The prohibition has existed since **2002** and is broken daily. Ley 8220 creates **no fund, no platform and no technical standard, and its only sanction route is disciplinary** **[V]** |

This is the gap in one line: **the duty exists and the mechanism does not.** Ley 8220 art. 2 forbids re-asking;
nothing obliges any institution to build the channel that would make compliance possible.

### 1.3 Institutions that would each have to connect

Each already runs a working digital system, separately. From `docs/research/legal-cr.md` §B **[V]**:

**TSE** (civil registry; IDC digital identity since Sept 2025) · **Hacienda** (TRIBU-CR since Oct 2025; SICOP under
Ley 9986) · **CCSS** (Oficina Virtual) · **Ministerio de Salud** (PSF, Decreto 43432-S) · **Registro Nacional**
(Crear Empresa) · **INS** (riesgos del trabajo, Código de Trabajo art. 193) · **CFIA** (APC) · **MEP** (EDUS/matrícula)
· **PROCOMER/COMEX** (VUI — 19 institutions, >40 trámites, **54 municipalities** by Feb 2025 **[V]**) ·
**82 municipalities**, each with its own Ley de Patentes.

**Ley 9986 (SICOP) is the drafting precedent that matters.** It is the one Costa Rican statute that makes a single
national platform mandatory for every public entity, on pain of nullity **[V]**. A once-only law could copy it
almost verbatim. That is a far more useful thing to point at than a general appeal to modernise.

### 1.4 The body that has not acted

**The Asamblea Legislativa.** No statute creates a once-only duty, a mandatory exchange layer, or an audit-log
requirement **[V]** (`docs/research/reference-laws-and-evidence.md` comparative matrix). Whether any bill is
currently filed **could not be established** — see §4.

## 2. What a resident can already do, alone, today

Nothing in this section requires a campaign, an organisation or anyone's permission. These are individual rights
already granted by Costa Rican law, exercisable by any *habitante* — the Constitution uses *todos*, *nadie* and
*habitantes* in these articles, not *ciudadanos* **[V]**.

| Mechanism | Basis | What it actually gets you | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Derecho de petición** | Constitución **art. 27** — petition before any public official, individually or collectively, with a right to *pronta resolución* | A written answer on the record from the institution. The answer, or the silence, is the evidence for everything below | Free |
| **Acceso a información de interés público** | Constitución **art. 30** | Documents, plans, timetables, budgets — including an institution's own interoperability plan under Ley 9943 art. 2 | Free |
| **Ley 8220 complaint** | Ley 8220 art. 2 (no re-asking), art. 7 (silencio positivo), before MEIC Mejora Regulatoria (art. 11, art. 13 criterio vinculante) | A binding criterion against the institution that asked for what the State already had | Free |
| **Contraloría de Servicios** | **Ley 9158**, Sistema Nacional de Contralorías de Servicios, coordinated by MIDEPLAN **[?]** — verify the article before publishing | Every institution must run one; it is the internal complaints route and the fastest to use | Free |
| **Recurso de amparo** | Constitución art. 48; Ley de la Jurisdicción Constitucional | The strongest instrument available. **No lawyer, no fee, no form** — a written page to the Sala Constitucional, at any hour | Free |
| **Defensoría de los Habitantes** | Ley 7319 **[?]** — verify | Investigates institutional inaction and publishes findings | Free |
| **Denuncia before the CGR** | Ley 7428 **[?]** — verify | Audit of how a public body is using its resources | Free |

### The precedent that shows this is not theoretical

**The ANGD's own reglamento exists because of a Sala IV order in July 2024** **[V]**. A statute passed in 2021 sat
unregulated for three years; a constitutional challenge produced Decreto 44636-MP-MICITT, and the agency became
operational in September 2025.

That is the whole answer to "how do constituents keep institutions accountable for inaction", and it is worth
stating plainly rather than abstractly: **in Costa Rica, an amparo against administrative inaction is free, needs no
lawyer, and has already moved this exact file once.**

## 3. What accountability looks like when it works

The instruments that already produce public, citable pressure — none of which requires anyone to organise anything:

- **CGR Índice de Transformación Digital** (DFOE-CAP-SGP-00002-2022): 267 institutions assessed; "la mayoría de
  instituciones se ubican en los niveles inicial y básico"; recommends "fortalecer la interoperabilidad" **[V]**.
  Press breakdown 78 inicial / 108 básico / 68 intermedio / 7 optimizado **[SECONDARY]**. This is a ready-made,
  official, per-institution scoreboard.
- **Ley 8220 art. 7 silencio positivo**, invocable by electronic document with firma digital **[V]** — an
  institution that does not answer in time can lose the point by default.
- **MEIC's Catálogo Nacional de Trámites** — the register against which a demanded requisito can be checked.

## 4. What could not be established, and why

**Two sources do not respond from this environment at all**, tested 8 September 2026:

| Source | Result |
|---|---|
| `asamblea.go.cr` | connection fails — no TCP |
| `gobiernodigital.go.cr` | connection fails — no TCP |
| `micitt.go.cr`, `cgr.go.cr`, `delfino.cr`, `nacion.com` | 200 |
| `pgrweb.go.cr/scij` | 302 |

This is the same failure class `denuncia.cr` documented for GitHub runners: several `.go.cr` hosts refuse TCP from
datacentre ranges while answering normally from an ordinary connection. It is **not** evidence that the sites are
down.

**Consequence, and it is the important one:** the single most decision-relevant fact — *whether a bill creating a
once-only duty is currently filed, and in which comisión it is sitting* — **cannot be automated and has not been
verified here.** It is scheduled as a manual browser check. Any claim on the public site about the state of
legislation must wait for that. Do not infer from the absence of search results that no bill exists; absence of
evidence from a source you cannot reach is not evidence of absence.

Reachable secondary indexes worth trying for the same information: `delfino.cr/asamblea/legislacion`,
`acontecer.co.cr/asamblea/expedientes`, `proyectos.conare.ac.cr/asamblea`.
